# Tidemark art bible

For SOL and anyone producing Tidemark illustrations. Generated by `tools/tidemark/checklist.py` (do not edit; change the script). The checklist decides what is drawn: never invent a card, a card type, a subject or an extra illustration.

## The look

Traditional painterly high fantasy with a nautical identity: illustrative, detailed and readable at card size, varied in subject, composition, light and atmosphere, consistent as one world without looking uniform.

- **Ordinary cards look ordinary.** A fisherman, soldier, dockworker, clerk or fox is drawn as an ordinary person or animal in an ordinary moment. Commons and Uncommons use natural light and plain, worn, practical clothing and gear.
- **No default epic.** No glowing eyes or auras, halos, dramatic rim lighting, monumental scale or ornate armour unless the card calls for it. Legendary cards may be more dramatic, but restrained: one strong light source, no excess ornament.
- **Magic is shown physically.** Water moves, weather turns, roots grow, a bell rings. Avoid abstract swirls and particle effects. The Abyssal Choir is the one faction where cold bioluminescence is normal, used sparingly.
- **Clear subjects and silhouettes.** One readable subject or a small readable group; the card's subject must be obvious at a glance.

## The art window and the grid

- Every cell is a complete landscape scene for the art window of a traditional Magic-style card: landscape 4:3 cell; keep the subject inside the central 80% so it survives cropping to the card art window.
- SOL generates 3x3 contact sheets with thin dark gutters. Each sheet holds **nine different card illustrations** (never nine poses of one subject), in the order listed in `SHEETS.md` (left to right, top to bottom).
- **Forbidden in the art:** card frames, portrait card mockups, borders, typography, titles, rules text, labels, numbers, watermarks, logos, UI. The game draws the frame, the rarity symbol and every word.
- Rarity never needs its own illustration or frame. Reprints reuse their original illustration and are never requested again. The ten anniversary alternate arts are separate, listed requests.
- Empty cells listed for a sheet stay empty (plain neutral grey). Never fill them with invented cards.

## Factions

- **Crownlands** (Land; frame colour royal blue, icon a tower): chalk cliffs, hedged coastal farmland, pale stone castles with blue slate roofs. Palette azure, bone white, slate grey, muted gold. Wool, padded cloth, mail and plain practical steel; heraldry of a blue tower on white.
- **Thornwild** (Land; frame colour forest green, icon a tree): old-growth coastal forest, moss, ferns, bracken, mist, stone circles. Palette moss green, bark brown, rust bracken, pale fog grey. Hide, bark, woven plant fibre, antler and bone; animals drawn as real animals.
- **Iron Reach** (Land; frame colour rust red, icon a mountain): basalt mountains, quarries, mines, foundries and sea-facing batteries. Palette charcoal, iron grey, ochre dust, ember orange. Leather aprons, iron scale, soot and grime; constructs are riveted iron and stone, heavy and mechanical.
- **Tideborn** (Sea; frame colour teal, icon waves): underwater cities of coral and pearl, kelp forests, lagoons and tidal flats. Palette teal, turquoise, pearl white, coral pink. Tideborn are sea-dwelling people with fin-like ears and subtle gill lines, in shell, kelp and woven fibre; light falls in shafts from the surface.
- **Free Fleets** (Sea; frame colour gold, icon a ship's wheel): working ships, busy quays, open sea, coves and harbour taverns. Palette weathered wood, canvas, sea blue, faded reds. Sailors in practical working clothes; traders and privateers, not cartoon pirates; ships drawn with believable rigging.
- **Abyssal Choir** (Sea; frame colour purple, icon a kraken): the deep ocean, trenches, sunken ruins and lonely night shores. Palette ink black, deep blue-violet, bone grey, with sparing cold bioluminescence (the one faction where glow is normal). Drowned cultists in sodden robes; creatures are deep-sea animals made vast and strange, unsettling rather than gory.
- **Harbor Leagues** (Dual; frame colour warm brown, icon scales): merchant ports, lighthouses, guild halls, canals and quays. Palette warm sandstone, brass, harbour blue, lamplight amber. Officials, clerks and dockworkers in tan and blue; busy, lived-in civic spaces.
- **Neutral** (Neutral; frame colour stone grey, icon a waystone): roads, inns, moors, weather and ruins that belong to no faction. Palette earthy neutrals with the sky and sea doing the colour work. Travellers, mercenaries and ordinary animals; weather drawn as real weather.

When an illustration shows faction heraldry (banners, shields, sails), use these icons and colours, never invented emblems.

## The card frame

The game draws every frame; SOL never paints one. The look follows the owner's card frame guide (`FRAME_GUIDE.md`, image in `reference/`): the illustration fills a 4:3 landscape window under the name bar, so compose for that window. The guide's placeholder rules text and symbols are not Tidemark's; `FRAME_GUIDE.md` lists the corrections.

## Composition by card type

| Type | Visual group | How to frame it |
|---|---|---|
| Units (people) | figures | One clear subject (or a small readable group) in a believable setting; full figure or three-quarter view in the middle of the frame, with room above the head and at both sides for cropping |
| Units (animals, constructs, monsters) | creatures | The creature as the clear subject in its habitat with a readable silhouette and the whole body visible; for big creatures include a small scale cue (a boat, a person, a tree) |
| Spells and Enchantments | scenes | The moment the effect happens, readable at a glance: who or what acts and what changes. Keep it physical (water, weather, people, objects); no abstract magic swirls |
| Relics | objects | The object as the clear subject in a real setting (on a bench, a rack, a table or in use), not floating on a plain background; it fills about half the frame |
| Vessels | ships | The ship in three-quarter view on the water with the waterline and full rig visible; sea and sky set the weather and the scale |
| Strongholds | architecture | The structure seen whole from a distance, with small figures, carts or boats for scale and its surroundings visible |
| Domains | landscape | A wide establishing view of the place with no central character; it should read as somewhere a battle could be fought |

## Register by rarity

| Rarity | Register |
|---|---|
| Common | Ordinary register: natural light, muted colour, worn and practical details; no glow, halo or magical light unless the card itself is magic |
| Uncommon | Ordinary register: natural light and grounded details; magic only where the card is magic, and then shown simply |
| Rare | Elevated register: a more considered composition and light, still grounded and believable |
| Legendary | Legendary register: the most dramatic composition this game allows, but restrained: one strong light source, no excessive ornament, no glowing halos |

## Eras

Ten fictional years of art share one painterly style. Era shows in the content, not the brushwork: Founding-era cards (2016-2017) have the simplest gear, ships and buildings; later eras add bigger ships, deeper ruins and more elaborate fortifications.

## How each cell is described

Every cell in `SHEETS.md` and `art_queue.json` names the card ID, the illustration ID, the exact subject and the direction: the faction look, the framing for its type, the register for its rarity and, when the subject does not already set it, the light. The asset filename is fixed per illustration (`tidemark-production/art/TMI-####-title.png`).
