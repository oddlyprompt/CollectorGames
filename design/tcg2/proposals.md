# Second TCG: proposals for the open decisions

Answers to section 9 of `brief.md`. **Everything here is a proposal for the owner to approve, change or reject.** Nothing is built, and no cards are generated, until the owner signs off (brief, section 10). Written 2026-10-16 by Claude Code.

## What the existing engine already gives this game

- **Card identity versus printing** is already separate in the Wildkin engine: every printing has its own key and price, reprints share one gameplay identity (`cid`), and the price model already discounts play value by how many times a card has been printed. Original printings keeping a premium is a small extension.
- **Simulated competition:** the Wildkin rules engine plays about 1,300 games a second in the browser, builds decks, finds the strongest field, runs Swiss tournaments with a played-out final, and bans cards. The same pattern works here with a new rule set.
- **History at the start of a game:** the Comics game generates eighty years before you arrive; Wildkin plays its metagame forward from release. Ten years of this game is in the same range.
- **Finding old cards:** the Hunt (garage sales, collections, shops) exists in the Sports and Comics games and can stock this game's old cards and sealed product.
- **Art intake:** the 3x3 sheet checker, cutter and approval tools work for full-scene illustrations as well as cutouts.

## 2. Core rules

**Proposal: two fronts.** The table has two lanes, the **Shore** (Land) and the **Deep** (Sea). Units enter their home front. Any unit can attack the other front (universal combat), but crossing is a tactical cost: a crossing attacker strikes with 1 less power unless something carries it (a Vessel carries Land units out to sea; Sea units with *Surge* hit the Shore at full strength). Strongholds stand on the Shore and protect it; Domains change one front. This makes Land and Sea mechanical without locking either out.

- **Deck:** 50 cards, at most 3 copies of a card (Legendary cards: 1).
- **Winning:** each player has a **Harbor** with 20 strength. Reduce the opponent's Harbor to 0. A player who must draw from an empty deck loses.
- **Turn:** Rise (Influence capacity +1, refill), Draw, Main (play cards), Battle (attack, then a second Main), End.
- **Units:** Power and Toughness; damage heals at end of turn. Defenders choose blockers from the attacked front.
- **Speed:** most Spells are played on your own turn; Spells marked *Swift* can be played on the opponent's turn (the counters and tricks).
- Target game length: 8 to 10 turns each.

Alternative if two fronts feel too complex: one shared battlefield, with Land/Sea only as a keyword and synergy layer.

## 3. Influence and Allegiance

Influence as in the brief: capacity +1 per turn to a maximum of 10, refilled each turn, no resource cards.

Allegiance, two options:

- **A. Commitment (proposed).** Your **Allegiance** is the number of Land or Sea permanents you control. A card marked *Sea 2* needs 2 Sea permanents in play; Neutral cards need nothing; Dual cards accept Land or Sea. Dedicated decks reach their strongest cards sooner; hybrid decks can play both sides but later.
- **B. The Tide.** One shared meter from Land 5 to Sea 5 that moves when either player commits to a side; cards are stronger when the Tide favours them. More dramatic, harder to read and harder to balance.

## 4. The seven card types

| Type | Proposed role |
|---|---|
| **Units** | Fighters with Power and Toughness, Land or Sea or Dual |
| **Spells** | One-time effects; *Swift* spells work on the opponent's turn |
| **Relics** | Permanent objects; some attach to a unit as equipment |
| **Vessels** | Enter the Deep; carry Land units (they cross without penalty), move units between fronts, can be attacked |
| **Strongholds** | Enter the Shore; absorb attacks aimed at the Harbor (an attack must go through them first), give defensive bonuses |
| **Domains** | One per front; change that front for both players (a Reef slows crossings, a Mountain Pass favours defenders) |
| **Enchantments** | Ongoing magic on a front, a unit or a player |

## 6. Rarity and set composition

- Rarities: Common (circle), Uncommon (diamond), Rare (star), Legendary (crown). Rarity is shown by the symbol and its colour; frames stay the same.
- Premium printings: Foil (any rarity) and, rarely, an Anniversary alternate art.
- Standard set: 200 cards (100 Common, 60 Uncommon, 30 Rare, 10 Legendary).
- Pack: 12 cards (8 Common, 3 Uncommon, 1 Rare; Legendary replaces the Rare about 1 pack in 8; one Foil about 1 pack in 6). Box: 36 packs.

## 7. History: eras and representative sets

Ten years, four eras, about 30 historical sets in the timeline. **Only representative cards are created.** Each historical set gets about 40 to 60 cards (its rares, Legendaries, famous commons and the cards that mattered in play); the rest of each set exists as unnamed bulk that keeps pack contents and prices believable.

| Era | Years | Character |
|---|---|---|
| Founding | 1 to 2 | the core game; simple cards, now the most valuable original printings |
| Expansion | 3 to 5 | Vessels and Strongholds grow up; the first famous bans |
| The Deep | 6 to 8 | the Abyssal Choir, Domains, the power peak |
| Modern | 9 to 10 | balance era, reprints, the lead-up to the anniversary |

About 500 historical cards in total, added in batches.

## 8. The anniversary set

- 220 cards: 150 new, 70 reprints, of which 10 are reprints with new anniversary art.
- Announced on day one of a new game, released a few weeks in (like First Bond), so the player sees the hype build.
- Anniversary sealed products (a collector box) as a chase.

## 9. Reprint policy

- Standard sets: about 15% reprints. Anniversary and special sets: about 30%.
- Reprints use the original art. Original printings keep a collector premium; a reprint lowers the play value of every printing of that card, but less for first printings.
- Some cards are never reprinted (an in-world "reserve list" of about 20 Founding-era cards), which gives the old market its grails.

## 10. Art and card frames

Workload: about 150 new anniversary illustrations plus about 500 historical ones, about 650 pictures, about 73 sheets of nine. Batches by era make it manageable, and art-pending placeholders keep the game playable while it arrives.

Art checklist for each sheet: one card per cell, subject named in the queue, readable silhouette at card size, no text or symbols, landscape cell ready to crop to the card's art window, ordinary subjects look ordinary.

Frames are drawn by the game: frame colour by allegiance (Land: stone and bronze; Sea: deep teal and silver; Dual: split; Neutral: grey), a type icon, the set symbol in the rarity colour, Influence cost and Allegiance pips at the top.

## 11. Competition and market rules

- **Two formats.** *Standard* (sets from the last two years) and *Eternal* (every card, with a banned list). Eternal is what keeps old cards worth something in play, not just to collectors.
- A rules engine like Wildkin's plays real games for the current Standard field. History is simulated with a lighter deck-strength model per era, precomputed when the game is built.
- Value factors: printing era, rarity and print run, play in Standard and Eternal, reprint history, collector demand per faction and famous card, bans, and historical significance (cards that won a championship carry a lasting premium).

## 12. Release cadence

- Three main sets a year (spring, summer, autumn) and one supplemental product a year.
- Standard rotation once a year, dropping the oldest year of sets.

## Order of decisions

The rules shape everything else, so they come first:

1. Name (1) and the two-front board (2): the biggest choices.
2. Allegiance (3) and the seven card types (4).
3. Factions (5) and rarity (6).
4. History plan (7, 8, 9).
5. Art and frames (10), competition (11) and cadence (12).

Once 1 to 4 are approved, Claude Code can build a small playable core (about 60 cards) and run it through the simulator to test the 45 to 55% matchup target, before any large card library is made.
