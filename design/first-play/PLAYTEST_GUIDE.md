# Trading Card Collector: first-play guide (closed alpha)

For a session of 45 to 60 minutes. Nobody has played this build yet; you are among the first. There are no wrong moves and nothing you do can break anything that matters.

This page has two parts: one for the person playing, one for the person running the session. If you are doing both, read the player part only until you have finished playing.

---

## For the player

### What this is
You collect trading cards in a small town. There are three card games (Wildkin, Tidemark and Broken Reach), shops that sell them, an online marketplace, and a calendar. You decide what to collect and how. The game does not tell you what is a good deal. Finding out is the game.

### Before you start
- Use a phone if you can (that is what it is made for). A computer works too.
- Open the link you were given. Choose **New game** and keep the first choice of start (Classic).
- Sound is not part of this build. Nothing is missing if it is silent.

### How time works
Nothing happens until you say so. **Next day** moves the world on by one day; **Skip** jumps to the next thing that matters. You get pocket money on Fridays. Take as long as you like on any day.

### A suggested path (follow it loosely, or not at all)
1. **First ten minutes: look around.** Read what Home tells you. Open each tab once. Move a few days forward. If something is unclear, note it and keep going.
2. **Then: get some cards.** When a game has packs on a shelf, buy a little and open it. Look at what you got.
3. **Then: go to town.** Open **Stores**. Visit two or three different shops and one big-box store. Look at **Out & about**.
4. **Then: pick something to want.** A set, a favourite card, a kind of card, a shop to be known at. Spend the rest of the time going after it.
5. **Last five minutes:** stop wherever you are and fill in the feedback form.

You do not have to sell anything, grade anything or make money. You may if you want to.

### While you play
Please say out loud, or jot down, the moments when you:
- did not know what to do next,
- were surprised (pleasantly or not),
- wanted something the game did not let you do,
- felt like stopping, or felt like you could not stop.

Those moments are worth more to us than any score.

### If something goes wrong
- The screen stops responding for a few seconds after **Next day**: wait up to twenty seconds. It can happen at the start of a game week. Note the date shown at the top.
- Anything looks broken: take a screenshot if you can, note the date at the top of the screen, and carry on.
- You are stuck: tell the person running the session. That is useful to know, not a failure.

## For the person running the session

### Setting up
- Hand the player a link to the release candidate (how to serve it: `design/first-play/DEPLOYMENT.md`). Do not use the public site; it is an older build.
- Note the build shown in the browser tab title (for example `0.9.0-alpha-rc1`), the device and the browser.
- A fresh browser profile, or a private window, so no earlier save is offered.
- Have a timer. Start it when the launcher appears.

### During the session
- **Do not explain the game, and do not answer "is this a good price?"** If the player asks what to do, ask what they think they can do. Only step in if they have been stuck for two minutes; write down where.
- Write down the time of: first purchase, first pack opened, first visit to Stores, first look at Out & about, first time they say what they want to collect, any moment they go quiet for long.
- Write down their words when they react to something, as close to exactly as you can.
- Note every wait longer than about three seconds after Next day, with the date on screen.
- Do not hint at what cards are worth, where bargains are, what a pack might hold, or how any price will move. If you know, keep a straight face.

### What we most want to learn
1. Does a new player understand, without help, what they can do in the first ten minutes?
2. Is opening a pack satisfying?
3. Do the shops feel like different places?
4. Do yard sales and the card show feel like something worth getting up for?
5. Does the player form a goal of their own, and does the game help them chase it?
6. What is tedious?
7. Would they play again tomorrow?

### After the session
- Ask the player to fill in `FEEDBACK_FORM.md` (on paper or in a copy of the file) before you discuss anything.
- Then ask the two open questions at the end of the form aloud and write down the answers.
- From Settings, use **Export backup** and keep the file with your notes: it lets us open the exact world the player was in.
- Record facts and quotes. Do not summarise what you think they felt.
