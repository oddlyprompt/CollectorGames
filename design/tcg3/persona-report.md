# Rift Parade: three kinds of deck builder

Generated by `node tools/rift-parade/personas.js` and `personas_report.js` on 2026-10-18. Each deck was tested against the eight representative decks (40 games per opponent, seats alternating, representatives piloted by the "balanced" player), so each deck has 320 games behind its win rate (one standard error about 2.5 points). Decks were also played against each other (12 games per pairing of decks). **Rarity gives no gameplay strength**, so "the best cards" here means the rarest, which is exactly what the rich collector tests.

| Builder | How the decks were made | Pilot | Decks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Expert synergy builder | Random 2-3 theme packages from all 130 cards with a low curve, then 8 rounds of swapping 1-3 cards and keeping a change only if it did at least as well against the field (6 games per opponent per test) | balanced | 6 |
| Rich collector (best cards, weak pilot) | Every Secret Rare, then Super Rares in 2s and 3s, then Rares; no curve planning | greedy (no look-ahead) | 6 |
| Budget combo player (Commons and Uncommons only) | The same search as the synergy builder, but only Commons and Uncommons (84 cards) | balanced | 6 |

## What this shows

* **Rarity does not buy wins, as intended.** The rich collector's piles of Secret, Super and Rare cards averaged 7% against the field (best deck 14%). Switching him to the good pilot only moved the average to 9%, so the problem is the deck: a mean cost of 4.9 means he cannot cast his cards (slipping one card per turn gives roughly one energy per turn), not that he plays them badly. Even the budget player beat him 67% of the time.
* **The budget player was weak on average (17%) but one deck reached 34%** (`budget4`, Mark and disruption). With only Commons and Uncommons the search had 84 cards to work with and 8 swaps; cheap decks are viable but did not match the hand-tuned archetypes.
* **The synergy builder found two decks at or above the field**: `synergy3` (58%) and `synergy2` (54%). `synergy3` overlaps 50% with Resonance Chorus (Sever and Mend added to the Resonance core), so it is a strong variant rather than a new idea. `synergy2` (disruption, Kinetic, Rift; 33% overlap with Rift Walkers) is the genuinely novel list that held up. The other four synergy decks finished at 20-29%.
* **No degenerate deck appeared.** Nothing above 58%, nothing that beats the whole field, and no repeated card pair that dominated. The cards the synergy builder reached for most (Unsealed Service Corridor in 5 of 6 decks, Bondbreaker Stroke 4 of 6) are cheap utility cards, not a combo.
* Head to head: the synergy builder beat both others (81% against the rich collector, 76% against the budget player).

## Limits

The "expert" is a random-restart hill climber with 8 swaps per deck and 6 decks (about 15,000 games in total), not a human or a long search; a real expert would very likely do better, so 58% is a floor for what synergy building can reach. "Value" is the collector rarity, because pricing is out of scope. The rich collector's pilot is the no-look-ahead "greedy" player, which is a modelling choice for "not good at deploying". Win rates against the field use 40 games per opponent (320 per deck, standard error about 2.5 points for the final numbers, larger for the head-to-head cells).
