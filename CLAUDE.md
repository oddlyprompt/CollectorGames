# Collector games: guide for Claude Code

## Wildkin Version 1: First Bond and Duskfall come from the manifests
`design/wildkin-v1-production-bible.md` (the owner's production bible) is the source of truth. The prototype creatures, sets and art are gone from the game. The Wildkin build reads **only** `wildkin-production/manifest.json` (set 1, the 180 First Bond cards), `wildkin-production/manifest-dsk.json` (set 2, the 180 Duskfall cards) and `wildkin-production/approved/registry.json` (approved art):
- **Duskfall** (`tools/wildkin_dsk.py`, `--check` like the other): built from the owner's checklist in SOL's handoff (`wildkin-production/sets/dsk/art_manifest.json`) plus the design tables in the script. Names, numbers, affinities, families and which cards are reprints are the checklist's; rules for the 104 new cards are **designed** and rarities, fandom, biome effects and the release date (7 December 2026) are **decided** (2026-10-17), not canon. The 27 reprints and 9 Essence copy their First Bond card and play as it (`ident`: same `cid`, so the 4-copy rule and the field treat them as one card). Card reference: `wildkin-production/sets/dsk/RULES.md`. First Bond's manifest is never touched by it.
- `tools/wildkin_manifest.py` builds the manifest, art queue and reports from `wildkin-production/source/appendix_a_roster.tsv` (extracted from the bible) and the tables in the script. Every fact has a status: **canon**, **designed** (card rules, 2026-10-15), **decided** (non-mascot affinities, non-creature rarities, fandom weights, decided under the owner's instruction of 2026-10-15) or **provisional** (nothing left in First Bond; kept for future sets). Never present provisional as canon, and never invent cards, names or numbers. `--check` fails if the files are stale or invalid. It also writes `wildkin-production/RULES.md`, the readable rules and card reference.
- `tools/wildkin_design.py` holds the Version 1 card design: every attack, ability and trainer effect as small operations ("ops"), the weakness wheel, the decided affinities and the fandom weights. Card text is generated from the ops, so text and play never disagree.
- `src/engine/tcg_rules.js` (`WKR`) is the rules engine: full games (setup, Essence, evolution, abilities, Allies/Gear/Tactics/Biomes, conditions, weakness, prizes), a greedy player for both sides, a deck builder, and the field (candidate decks, gauntlet, round robin). It runs in the browser and in Node. `tools/wildkin_meta.js` precomputes the field at build time (`WK_META`; without Node the game computes it). `tools/wildkin_sim.js` is the balance check: run it after any card or engine change.
- The metagame (`tcg_world.js`) uses the engine's matchups: weekly share shifts, Swiss tournaments with a top-8 bracket and a final played card by card, and a rules committee that bans the card whose loss hurts a dominant deck most (tested by rebuilding the deck), never a staple. Saves keep the field they use (`G.tcg.mx`) with a fingerprint of the base field, so a change to card rules or the engine rebuilds it on load; bump `RV` in `tcg_rules.js` only for a change the fingerprint cannot see.
- The field is alive (`tcBrewWeek`, `tcPruneWeek`, constants `TCM` in `tcg_world.js`): most weeks there is a chance (higher in the six weeks after a release, when one deck has led for six weeks, and late in a format) that players try four candidates (known lists nobody plays, re-tuned versions of established decks with a trainer package from `TC_TECH`, rogue builds around other final forms; `buildDeck(..., tune)`), test each with the engine against the six most played decks, and bring the best one if it holds up. A new list plays up to 5 points worse for its first six weeks (`tcRough`). A deck under 0.8% for six weeks leaves (12 to 22 decks) and is not rediscovered for a year (`G.tcg.gone`). After a ban the current field is rebuilt; after a release the new field keeps the old decks still above 3%. Packed decks carry `aid` and `tn` (tech name) so versions of one lead and partner are different decks.
- `tools/wildkin_art.py` is the only way art gets into the game (check, approve, reject, ingest, legacy for 3x3 sheets; singles-check and singles-approve for single premium paintings mapped by `incoming/premium-individuals/card-map.json`). Approved art is never overwritten without `--replace`. SOL (the art model) follows `wildkin-production/SOL_HANDOFF.md` and owns art direction: no concept approval step; a subject's first approved sheet is its reference.
- `tools/wildkin_build.py` (called by `build.py` for tcg) writes `WK_SETS` (one canon per set; `WK_CANON` is set 1), `WK_METAS` (the field per group of sets in Standard: `"1"`, `"1,2"`) and `WK_ART` into game.js and copies only approved images to `dist/tcg/art/`. Missing art draws a labelled "ART PENDING" placeholder, never another card's picture.
- Opening (`tcg_ui2.js`): several packs or a box open to a grid of pack tiles; a pack glows when its best card is premium or worth `TC_GOOD` ($5), gold at `TC_BIG` ($50). Inside a pack the Essence is revealed first and the rare slot last; good cards are sleeved and top-loaded; big hits and Gem Mint grades call `celebrate()` (drama.js, shared by all games). The sealed list groups identical products with Open all.
- Stores (`tcg_stores.js`): big-box retail (restocks Mondays, `tcShopWeek`/`tcShopDay`), The Tidepool local game store (sealed over list, Thursday allocation, a weekly singles case, a buylist at 55% of market; `G.tcg.lgs`) and Kinvault online singles (every print near mint at market +15%, weekly stock; `G.tcg.kv`). Store state is created on first use, so old saves need no migration.
- Collecting (`tcg_collect.js`): optional goals with progress on Home (premium pull, first sale, every affinity, a family of three or more, Gem Mint, a box held a year, base set, every premium, master set; `G.tcg.goals`), a wantlist with the cheapest source across Kinvault, The Tidepool and the marketplace (`G.tcg.want`), Missing and Wantlist filters in the set checklist, mark-all-seen, sell duplicates to the buylist (keeps your best copy), and news lines that say when they touch your cards or sealed product. Opening has a per-save switch to hide the pack glow (`G.tcg.opt.nohint`), and the summary compares the pulls with what was paid and with the sealed value given up.
- Net worth (journal, save list) is cash plus `netWorthNow()`, which includes Wildkin cards and sealed product.
- Ally cards draw the hero large on an approved scene chosen in `ALLY_BACKDROP` (`tools/wildkin_build.py`; presentation only, not in the manifest). The build trims cutouts to their subject box (`bb` in `WK_ART`). Non-creature illustrations keep the top of the painting (faces).
- Later sets: hidden until announced (`tcAnnounced`, 35 days ahead), then previews and preorders; on release day the field switches to the next `WK_METAS` group. A save already past a release date it never saw gets that release when it is opened (`tcNormalize`). Biome effects are data (`['biome', affinity, effect, amount]`; First Bond's name only the affinity, see `BIO_DEF` in `tcg_rules.js`). Packaging renders are per set (`product.fbd.*`, `product.dsk.*`; Duskfall has a pack and a closed box), lettered by the renderer with per-set positions and ink in `TC_PROD_LAYOUT`; a set without renders gets a drawn wrapper (`TC_PACK_ART`).
- Saves carry `G.tcg.cv=2`. Prototype saves (no `cv`) are labelled in the launcher, never loaded, never modified (`pack_apply.js`).
- Status and gaps: `wildkin-production/reports/V1_READINESS.md`, `outstanding_assets.md`, `reconciliation.md`.

## Tidemark: the second card game inside the card-game world (Tenth Tide and ten years of history)
Added 2026-10-19 (owner: "add Tidemark to the collector game like Wildkin: sets, packs, prices, market and the living metagame"). The cards, sets and art are `tidemark-production/` (manifest, `cards.json`, approved art) and the rules are `tools/tidemark/v1` (Version 1: **designed**, not canon). Nothing in the game invents a card, name or number. **It is not a separate game.** The `tcg` build (now "Trading Card Collector") holds Wildkin and Tidemark in one world: one save, one wallet, one marketplace (listing type `x`, keys `X|set|no|v`, NPC collector types `xpl xtl xtm xfl xsl xgr`), one grading queue (`G.player.xsubs`, rv kind `x`), one Home and one Collection (game chips Wildkin/Tidemark), and a Tidemark tab next to Wildkin (`VIEWS.xt` in `pack_apply.js`). State is `G.xtg` (world) and `G.xtc` (copies); a Wildkin save opened after this change gets the Tidemark world (and Tenth Tide's release and the weeks since) on load (`upgradeXt`). Scenarios: `tmbinder`, `tmbox` next to the Wildkin ones.
- **Code is a fork of the Wildkin card-game code** in `src/engine/tm/` with every name prefixed `xt`/`XT_` (Wildkin's are `tc`/`TC_`), so both load in one script without clashing; shared hooks (market, listing, faces, 3D viewer, journal, drama) dispatch on item type `x`: `tm_rules.js` (`TMR`, the engine in the browser: packed decks, unpacking, games, brewing), `tm_data.js` (sets, printings, keys), `tm_world.js` (the field, events, bans, previews, retail), `tm_value.js` (copies, prices, packs, sealed, NPC collectors), `tm_boot.js`, `tm_render.js`, `tm_ui1/2/3.js`, `tm_stores.js`, `tm_collect.js`. A fix to shared market behaviour usually has to be made in both `tcg_*` and `tm_*`. Before adding a global to either fork, check the other for the name (a function declared twice silently overwrites, a const twice is a SyntaxError). `tools/tidemark_build.py` writes `TM_SETS`, `TM_CARDS`, `TM_PR`, `TM_ART`, `TM_LIB` and the engine (`ops.js`, `engine.js`, `decks.js` wrapped as browser modules) in front of the game code, and copies the approved art re-encoded at q80 to `dist/tidemark/art/` (about 30 MB). Card faces are drawn by `tools/tidemark/v1/render.js` on a canvas the first time a card is shown and kept as a picture inside the card's SVG (`tmImgUrl`), so wear, foil, slabs and the 3D viewer work unchanged.
- **Sets:** 31, oldest first (First Tide = 1 ... Tenth Tide = 31, `TM_TT`). Release dates of the 30 historical sets are the third Friday of April, July or October of their year (decided); Tenth Tide is **26 June 2026** (decided, a few weeks into a new game that starts 1 June; announced on day one with the Legendaries and alternate arts shown, the rest revealed by 19 June, preorders open). Only the catalogued cards of a historical set exist (2,261 since 2026-10-24: **every historical set has 70 to 125 catalogued cards**, First Tide 125 of its advertised 250; the rest of the advertised run is not a product): **there is no bulk and no invisible filler** (2026-10-23, owner: content first). Cards are added to a set by a *wave* (`tools/tidemark/checklist_supp.py`): new `TMC-0651+` identities and `TMI-0651+` illustrations, numbers taken from free gaps in the set's checklist, art sheets numbered after every older sheet, so no existing id, number or sheet ever changes. First Tide and Reefward are hand-written there; every other set is one compact file `tools/tidemark/wave_<code>.py` with one line per card (checklist row and design together; `wave()` in `checklist_supp.py`), registered automatically, and `checklist.py` writes their designs to `tools/tidemark/v1/design_waves.json`, which `cards.js` reads. `python3 tools/tidemark/wave_plan.py CODE` lists a set and how many cards of each rarity it needs to reach 40 Commons, 16 Uncommons and 15 Rares or Legendaries. The art sheets are `TM-S078` to `TM-S286` (209 supplement sheets); SOL delivered every sheet through S147 (First Tide to Return of the Tide, 2026-10-24 and 25); the twenty later waves (sheets after S147, 1,164 cards) are **pending** (ART PENDING frames). A card is one identity (`cid` = the checklist's TMC number) with a printing per set; the 70 reprints in Tenth Tide share the identity of an older printing, so play value is shared and older printings keep a collector premium that grows with age (originals more than reprints; the 19 reserve-list cards most). The 10 alternate arts (TT 221-230) are variant `A`; every other printing comes regular (`N`) and foil (`F`).
- **Packs and prices (decided, not canon):** pack 15 cards, a classic booster (2026-10-21, owner: "craft it from classic boosters"): 10 commons, 3 uncommons, 1 rare slot (a Legendary replaces the rare about 1 pack in 8; an alternate art about 1 pack in 60 in Tenth Tide) and a 15th card that is one more common or, about 1 pack in 4, a foil of any rarity (60/28/10/2% C/U/R/L); every card is a real catalogued card and no card repeats inside a pack; box 36 packs (`XT_PACK`, `XT_BOX`, `TM_ODDS`, `xtRollPack`). **Honest products (2026-10-23):** a booster exists only for a set whose catalogue has at least 40 Commons, 15 Uncommons and 10 Rares or Legendaries (`xtBoosterSet`); since the waves of 2026-10-24 every set qualifies. A set below the thresholds would have no sealed product and would enter the world as one copy of each of its cards (`xtRollUnit`). The shared market kit has an optional market-size hook (`L.marketScale`, Tidemark 3.4: listings, Saltmarket stock and the clean-up grow with the catalogue; Wildkin 1). Reuse of existing identities as later reprints is audited in `design/tidemark-reuse-audit.md` (not applied). Retail $4.99 / $144 (the Lantern Room, the local game store, $5.49 / $159.99; Saltmarket is the online singles shop). Only Tenth Tide is printed (14 months, restocked Mondays, then out of print and rising); every older set is out of print, its sealed product only on the marketplace and priced up with age. Standard before Tenth Tide is the sets of the last two years (play value from the card's rating alone, announced to rotate); once Tenth Tide is out Standard is its 220 cards alone and every other card is Eternal-only. Prices: `TC_RB`, `TC_K`, `tcFairRaw` in `tm_value.js`; measure with `python3 tools/tidemark_econ.py [months]`.
- **The living metagame:** the engine plays a game in about 25 ms, so the decks and how they match up are precomputed: `node tools/tidemark/v1/library.js` (about 15 minutes, `--check` verifies it) writes `tidemark-production/meta_library.json`: about 180 decks (10 tuned archetypes, re-tuned versions, brews, build-arounds, and answers refined against the opening field) and the full matchup matrix. The game opens Tenth Tide with the 15 start decks and then runs the same weekly model as Wildkin (`tcMetaWeek`, `tcBrewWeek`, `tcPruneWeek`, `tcBanCheck`, constants `TCM`) with the library as the pool of lists players try; **finals, ban tests and decks rebuilt after a ban are played live** (a ban takes about five seconds to decide). Regenerate the library after any card, engine or `decks.js` change (the fingerprint makes saves rebuild their field on load).
- **Tests:** build `tcg`, then `python3 tools/tidemarktest.py` (catalog against `cards.json`, art, packs and odds, timeline, economy, play loop, engine, field after 14 months, ban path, screens, 3D viewer, saves), `python3 tools/tidemark_econ.py 36` (prices and `META` line), then `shelltest.py tcg`, `wildkintest.py`, `duskfalltest.py` (both games share the build). Status, decisions and gaps: `tidemark-production/GAME.md`.

### Treatments (2026-10-28; owner: "more chase cards without more art")
Three extra versions of a Tidemark card, **decided, not canon**, no new art: **Prismatic foil** `Z` (any card; replaces the 15th card in about 1 pack in 36, about one per box), **Gold stamp** `G` (Rare or Legendary; the rare slot in about 1 pack in 150) and **Serial-numbered** `S` (Legendary; about 1 pack in 2,500; each copy is numbered 1 to 100 from its id, `xtSerial`, not stored). Alternate arts have none. Odds in `TM_ODDS` (`prism`, `gold`, `serial`), prices in `XT_RB` (`x|Z`, `rare|G`, `leg|G`, `leg|S`), key lists `xtVariantsAll` / `xtAllKeysT`; `xtVariants` stays the base (regular, foil) that completing a set means. `xtFoilish(v)` is true for F, Z, G, S. Look: stronger rainbow for Z, gold border and sheen for G, gold plus a SERIAL-NUMBERED band and number in the inspector for S (`tm_render.js`). Tenth Tide pack worth about $5.7 (was $5.5). Tested by `tidemarktest.py` (rates over 150,000 packs, no repeats, price order, serial range). Looks only checked in code and tests, not by eye on a phone.

### Tidemark economy v2: the ten-year market (2026-10-20; design: `design/tidemark-economy-v2.md`)
Owner-approved (condition scarcity, real supply consequences, finite seller-backed Saltmarket, optional rebuild of older saves). **Decided by Claude, not canon.**
- **Versions.** A world carries `G.xtg.ecv===2` (`xtV2()`); worlds without it are the first economy (v1) and play exactly as before. New games and Wildkin saves that get their Tidemark world on load are v2. `window.__xtEcon='v1'` pins v1 (the golden and `tidemarktest.py` suites do, as regression for v1); an older Tidemark save can rebuild its vintage market from the Tidemark shop (a backup slot is made first, everything the player owns is untouched: `xtRebuildWithBackup`).
- **Code:** `src/engine/tm/tm_econ2.js` (parameters in `XT_E2`), small guarded branches in `tm_value.js` (price), `tm_stores.js` (Saltmarket, Lantern Room), `tm_data.js` (`xtV2`, condition price by age) and kit hooks (`gradeScarce`, `keep`, `cullSkip`, `culled`, `kvPriceReal`, `kvBuyReal`). The v2 code uses its own random streams (`withRng(mkRng(hsh(...)))`); the shared market code (listings, bids) still draws from the world's, so a v2 world's metagame follows the same rules but is not draw-for-draw the same as a v1 world's.
- **The replay** (`xtV2Replay`, about 0.1 s): for each historical set, packs opened (the real pack odds), sealed product hoarded, then year by year copies are lost, worn one band, graded or opened (holder types collector/player/casual, rarity multipliers), ending with the surviving copies held by collectors; 7% (more for valuable cards) go to attics (`o=-3`), grouped into estate lots and prize lots; every named card keeps at least one copy (in an attic). `G.xtg.ref/sm` hold each print's copy count at the start and a 45-day average; `G.xtg.led` is the ledger (made, lost, opened, graded, cull).
- **Daily** (`xtEconDay`): one thirtieth of the collectors' copies ages a month (loss, wear, collectors cracking sealed product); dealers buy copies from collectors into Saltmarket's real stock (`o=-2`, `kv.inv`) every Monday; estate sales, warehouse finds and prized binders (finite attic lots) and large collection sales list real copies with news lines. The clean-up (`xtCull`) never removes vintage copies.
- **Sealed (v2 worlds, historical sets):** a pack is worth the expected value of its contents times (1 + 0.06 x set prestige x years out of print, up to 12), never below half the retail price, then the scarcity factor; a box is 36 packs. Old sealed product is scarce on purpose (sealed scale 0.45, 60% of surviving sealed in attics, held by sealed collectors, not dealers): about 200 items in the world and under 1% of listings, instead of 900 and 11%. Measured box price over contents: 1.9x to 3.8x (a thin set with 10 named cards costs about $120 a box, First Tide about $1,100 to $2,000, its contents about $575). Thin sets (10 to 20 named cards) give the same few cards over and over because only named cards exist; that is a limit of the data, not a bug.
- **Prices:** v1 price times `xtV2Mult` (vintage NM premium by age and rarity, times (copies at start / smoothed copies now)^0.35, bounded to 0.5x-3x of v1); wear prices steeper with age; graded scarcity from the population report. Tenth Tide and sets under two years are exactly v1. Recent sets keep synthetic Saltmarket and Lantern Room stock; older prints only from real copies.
- **Measured (20 worlds for supply and condition, 3 for the rest):** about 2,780 raw copies visible at start across all historical sets (about 1,450 in sets over two years old); NM share of raw survivors: under 1 year 65%, 1-4 years 50%, 4-7 years 32%, 7-9 years 28%, 2016-era 22% (rares and Legendaries 19%). Over a year a 2016-2018 common or uncommon appears for sale 55% to 64% of the time (about 59%), an NM 2016-2018 rare or Legendary turns up about 1.3 to 1.5 times a year somewhere in the world, an NM 2016-2018 Legendary almost never. A collector with unlimited money gets 61% of the commons/uncommons of the oldest sets in a year, 28% of their Legendaries, 11% of their rares and Legendaries in NM after two years; 55% of all sets are complete at two years (any condition; the latest sets 100%, the oldest 11%). Over 36 months vintage supply stays flat (98%), the NM share falls from 33% to 27%, median vintage prices rise 30%, one scarce print can rise 4x. Tidemark's metagame in a v2 world: 5 leading decks and 11 in the top 5 over 24 months. Tests: `python3 tools/tidemark_econ2_test.py [ledger condition availability consequences prices completion long exploits saves]` (about 25 minutes for all; `tools/run_all_tests.sh --econ2`). Known and accepted: auction sniping at 55% of value wins a few auctions at about half price in both economies (older behaviour of the shared market, not changed); the player starts with $75 and the market has no vintage chase card the player can afford, by design.

### The Tidemark economy (measured with `tools/tidemark_econ.py`; re-measure after any change)
- Tenth Tide: pack worth about $5.30 in cards, $6.10 on launch week and about $5.40 a month later (retail $4.99), then $5.4 to $5.9 while it is on shelves; box about $205 at launch, $180 to $200 in print (retail $144). Out of print (from 26 Aug 2027) sealed rises with the years: box about $350 after two years, about $515 after three.
- Medians after launch: commons about 12 cents, uncommons about 30 cents, rares about $1.20, Legendaries about $5.30; foils 65 cents, $1.50, $3.60, $14.40; alternate art about $70 (the top one, Sovereign of the Lanes, about $96 on launch day then $70 to $80). Alternate arts are 10 cards in about 1 pack in 60.
- History: a First Tide box about $1,600; the most valuable single is about $235 raw (a foil Founding Legendary on the reserve list), which a GCS 10 multiplies; reserve-list cards are worth more than other Legendaries; older printings keep a collector premium that grows with age.
- Metagame over 36 months (`tidemark_econ.py 36` prints a META line; it varies by world): 6 to 7 different leading decks, 9 to 14 decks reaching the top 5, field 13 to 22 decks, 1 to 4 bans (a deck above 18% of the field for ten weeks), longest run at #1 about 8 to 13 months. The 18% ban line is lower than Wildkin's 24% because Crown Bastion settles near 17% to 20% and would otherwise lead for years.

## The Broken Reach (new franchise, started 2026-10-29; Play Alpha is playable, no collecting yet)
A space-opera card game with an 8-direction grid battler (combat rules v0.2, Set 01 "A Ship of Our Own", 180 cards, 27 alternate printings). Handoff: `broken-reach-production/source/`. Plan, open rules questions, AI notes and turn-order experiments: `broken-reach-production/PLAN.md`, `RULES_NOTES.md`, `TURN_ORDER_REPORT.md`, `SIM_REPORT_2026-10-29.txt`. Everything is proposed and provisional, nothing is canon or balanced.
- **Rules (2026-10-30):** the game offers four rule sets (`set01/rulesets.json`): *Tetra rules* (default: no pointing-back Guard bonus, combos stop after one level via `maxGen:1`, first-player bonus 2), *No combos* (`maxGen:0`), *Classic chains + fair start* and *Classic v0.2*. Measured in `broken-reach-production/TETRA_VARIANTS_REPORT.md` (`tools/brokenreach/variants.js`). Also in the engine, not offered: `auto` (a one-way arrow captures without a comparison; unplayable with 3.3 arrows per card).
- **Rules code (headless Node, no globals):** `tools/brokenreach/`: `import.js [--check]` (writes `set01/cards.json`; every ability text mapped by hand in `abilities.js`), `engine.js` (pure resolver; optional `rules`: komi, lastPassive, lastMinus, firstGuard, firstHand), `ai.js` (Easy/Standard/Hard; never sees the opponent hand), `presets.js` (three preset decks into `set01/presets.json`), `test.js` (62 golden tests), `sim.js`, `balance.js`, `balance_planets.js`, `ai_bench.js`.
- **Play Alpha (browser):** `src/br/br.js` and `br.css` are a separate page, not part of the collector script: `tools/brokenreach_build.py` (called by `build.py tcg`) wraps the engine and AI and writes `dist/tcg/broken-reach/` (index.html, br.js, br.css). The Trading Card Collector launcher links to it (`launcher.js`), and `build.py --publish` copies it with the rest of the tcg site. It stores only `localStorage['br_alpha_v1']` (custom decks, settings); it never touches the CollectorGames IndexedDB saves. All cards are unlocked for testing with placeholder art; the later collection and market integration will use their own prefixed fork (`src/engine/br/`), as Tidemark does.
- **Tests:** `node tools/brokenreach/test.js`; with `dist/` served, `python3 tools/brokenreach_uitest.py` (full matches against all three AIs on desktop and mobile sizes, rematch, deckbuilder, save separation, launcher entry).
- Not done: owned decks in the battler, career and tournaments, art, the 180-card balance pass, sound, a real-device test. (Collection, packs, shops and the market are done: see the collector-world section.)

### Broken Reach as a card line in the collector world (2026-10-30; owner: "I'm more worried about the collector experience")
The third card game in the Trading Card Collector, **decided by Claude, not canon**, same pattern as Tidemark but much smaller (one set, no metagame, no economy v2). Code in `src/engine/br/` (prefix `br`/`BR_`, item kind `b`, key `B|set|no|v`, state `G.brg` world and `G.brc` copies, grading queue `G.player.bsubs`, population `G.bpop`, NPC collector types `bpl btl bfm bfl bsl bgr`), loaded after Tidemark: `br_data.js` (catalogue, rarity, fandom, static play rating), `br_world.js` (previews 35 days out, release, retail lifecycle), `br_value.js` (prices, packs, sealed, preorders, opening, collectors, market kit config), `br_boot.js` (saves, daily tick, new and upgraded worlds, registers the line), `br_render.js` (card faces drawn as SVG with the eight arrows lit, labelled ART PENDING; packs, boxes, slabs, viewer hooks), `br_ui1/2/3.js` (tab, shop, set, collection, card pages, opening, marketplace, calendar), `br_stores.js` (big-box retail, The Cargo Cage local store, Waypoint Singles online), `br_collect.js` (goals, wantlist, duplicates). `tools/brokenreach_collect_build.py` writes `BR_SETS`, `BR_CARDS`, `BR_ALTS` from `broken-reach-production/set01/cards.json`. The tab list is in `src/packs/tcg.js`; Home, Collection and Market integration in `pack_apply.js` (`HAS_BR`).
- **Set 01 "A Ship of Our Own"** releases **14 August 2026** (decided), announced 35 days before (preorders, Legendaries, Super Rares and premium arts shown first). 180 base cards printed regular and foil (172 units, 8 planets) and 27 premium alternate arts (variant `A`: 16 Illustration, 8 Signature, 3 Eclipse Rare; a premium shares its base card's identity, `cid`). Printed 14 months (`BR_PRINT_DAYS` 425), restocked Mondays.

## What this is
Three browser games from one engine. All content is fictional.
- **Sports Card Collector** (`sports`): a simulated baseball league, card products, packs, grading, shows, breaks.
- **Comics Collector** (`comics`): eighty years of simulated comics, publishers, creators, variants, preorders, grading.
- **Wildkin Collector** (`tcg`): a fictional creature trading card game with sets, a tournament metagame, bans, rotation, a singles market and grading.
  It also holds **Tidemark**, a second card game in the same world: a land-and-sea game with ten years of history (30 sets), the tenth-anniversary set Tenth Tide, packs, a singles market, a living metagame and grading.

Plain HTML and JavaScript. No framework, no server, no build tool beyond one Python script.

## How the code is organised
- `src/engine/*.js` is the whole simulation and UI. `src/packs/*.js` defines each game (`PACK`). `src/shell/` is the launcher, save slots and per-game screens.
- **Everything is one global script.** `tools/build.py` concatenates files in a fixed order (see `ENGINE`, `TCG` lists), so load order matters. Later files often *wrap* earlier functions (`const _x=fn; fn=function(){...}`). A function name defined twice silently overwrites the first. Before adding a global, grep for the name. (A real clash happened with `evRow`.)
- `PACK` is defined first. `packHas('sports'|'comics'|'tcg'|'hunt')` decides which worlds get generated. `src/shell/pack_apply.js` sets the tabs, market and news filters, and the Wildkin home, shop and collection screens.
- **The baseball league only runs in the Sports build.** In comics and Wildkin, `newGame` calls `initNoLeague()` (empty players, products and standings) and the league, retail and release ticks do nothing (`packHas('sports')` gates in `market.js`, `world.js`). Old comics and Wildkin saves made before this still carry their frozen league data and load fine. Code shared with Sports (hunt, scenarios, market) must not assume baseball cards or products exist; gate with `HCARD()` or `packHas('sports')`.
- **Saves:** `src/shell/saves.js` stores named slots in IndexedDB, gzip-compressed JSON. Backups are `.ccsave` files (magic `CCS1`, a JSON header, then the payload). Keys starting with `_` are never saved. What gets saved is controlled by `saveReplacer` (market.js, cm_save.js, tcg_boot.js).
- **Determinism:** worlds come from `G.seed` through `mkRng(hsh(...))`. Wildkin cards come from the manifest (identical in every world); only hidden play-power jitter uses the seed. Nothing about cards is stored in saves.
- **Adding a new kind of collectible to the shared market** follows the pattern at the bottom of `tcg_value.js`: wrap `interest`, `itemOf`, `createListing`, `removeListing`, `transfer`, `recordSale`, `playerList`, `npcPool`, `itemLabel`, then add UI wrappers (see `tcg_ui3.js`: `listingRow`, `lsText`, `marketFiltered`, `listingSheet`, `designSheet`). Old code often assumes every item is a baseball card, so grep for `D(key)` and loops over `G.sales` when something crashes.
- The 3D inspector is `viewer3d.js` (three.js from `vendor/`). Print effects for close-ups are in `polish.js`. The grading reveal and crack-and-resubmit are in `drama.js`.

## The Wildkin economy (First Bond, measured with `tools/wildkin_econ.py`; re-measure after any change)
- First Bond launches on 8 June 2026, one week after a new game starts (1 June); it is announced with every card shown and preorders on day one. Launch prices carry a premium that fades in about a month (`nov` in `tcFairRaw`).
- Pack worth about $7 on launch day, about $5.40 a month later, then anchored near $5.20 while it is on retail shelves, against $4.50 retail. Box about $237 at launch, about $182 a month later, about $173 to $190 while in print, against $150.
- Retail lifecycle (`tcg_world.js`): a set is printed for `TC_PRINT_DAYS` (425 days; First Bond until 7 August 2027), announced 30 days ahead in the news. Restocks halve in the last two months and stop after; shelves sell down within weeks. While a set is on any shelf its sealed price is capped near retail (`tcSealedFair`, higher while it keeps selling out), so buying at retail and reselling earns only a few dollars. Out of print and off the shelves, sealed grows about 30% a year times prestige (box about $350 two years in, about $510 three years in), and singles get their out-of-print premium from the print end, not the release.
- Medians after launch: commons 3 to 4 cents, uncommons about 12 cents, rares about 40 to 50 cents, holo rares about $1.30, IR about $6 to $7, FA about $13, SIR about $200 to $220, Clasped about $130.
- Chase: Cactuft SIR about $540 on launch day, then about $400 to $430 raw (about $2,100 at GCS 10), then Nimbusail, Sel Veyra and June Kestrel SIRs around $200, Clasped Aegisopod about $190. Collector fandom (manifest, decided starting weights) bends prices through `TC_TUNE` in `tcg_value.js`; play value comes from how much the simulated decks use a card; the rare slot odds live in the manifest.
- Duskfall (from 7 December 2026, measured over 20 months): pack value $4.15 to $4.56, box $143 to $168 while in print (a little below First Bond); chase Koirune SIR and Clasped Velunox about $300; with both sets the top deck holds 14% to 21% and no ban triggered. Details: `wildkin-production/reports/DUSKFALL_STATUS.md`.
- Metagame movement over 36 months (`wildkin_econ.py 36` prints a META line): 10 different leading decks (3 before the living field), 27 decks reached the top 5 (15 before), field 16 to 27 decks, longest run at #1 eight months; prices unchanged.
- Metagame: 15 decks from the engine's field, simulated win rates about 39% to 63% in the round robin, top deck 13% to 21% of the field, the lead deck changes several times a year. With one set there is no rotation; bans only follow 10 weeks above 24% and did not trigger in a 12-month run (the ban path is tested in `wildkintest.py`).

## Source art
`art-source/` holds the owner's generated sheets, untouched (see `art-source/README.md`). They are the real art direction for the rebuilt Wildkin: creatures are soft, toy-like 3D renders on a flat screen; scenes are matching 3D miniature-diorama backgrounds with an open floor in the lower third. `python3 tools/build_art.py` cuts every sheet into `assets/creatures/<name>/<pose>.webp` and `assets/scenes/<biome>/<biome>-NN.jpg` and writes `assets/manifest.json` (scene biome, floor ground/water/air, time of day, tags; creature name, screen colour, pose). The scene tags and pose order live in `tools/build_art.py`. `tools/ingest.py` does the cutting: soft matte that keeps hairlines, screen colour un-mixed from edges, `--drop-effects` removes stray "!"/sparkle marks. `lab/index.html` is the art review page (every cutout on grey with edge-touching flags, and a creature-in-scene composer with light matching); `build.py --publish` copies it and the cut art to `docs/lab/`. Creature movement types and sizes (walker, swimmer, floater) live in `CREATURES` in `tools/build_art.py`. `START_HERE.md` has the first tasks.
